A reflexive verb (*wederkerend werkwoord*) has a pronoun that refers back to the subject, like English "I enjoy **myself**". In dictionaries they're listed with *zich*: *zich vergissen* (to be mistaken), *zich herinneren* (to remember). Many Dutch reflexive verbs are not reflexive in English, so they're worth learning with *zich*.

## The pronouns

| subject | reflexive | example: zich vergissen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ik | me | ik vergis me |
| jij / je | je | jij vergist je |
| u | zich (or u) | u vergist zich |
| hij / zij / het | zich | hij vergist zich |
| wij | ons | wij vergissen ons |
| jullie | je | jullie vergissen je |
| zij | zich | zij vergissen zich |

You'll also see *mij* instead of *me*, which is a bit more formal: *Ik vergis mij*. With *u*, both *zich* and *u* are correct; *u* is more common in Belgium.

## Word order

The reflexive pronoun comes right after the conjugated verb, or right after the subject if the subject comes after the verb.

- Sorry, ik vergis me. — Sorry, I'm mistaken.
- Ik voel me niet zo lekker vandaag. — I don't feel very well today.
- Voel je je al beter? — Are you feeling better yet?
- Morgen scheer ik me. — I'll shave tomorrow.
- Ik blijf thuis, omdat ik me niet goed voel. — I'm staying at home because I don't feel well.

Yes, *Voel je je* has *je* twice: the first is the subject, the second is the reflexive pronoun.

## Common reflexive verbs

| verb | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| zich vergissen | to be mistaken |
| zich herinneren | to remember |
| zich voelen | to feel |
| zich schamen (voor) | to be ashamed (of) |
| zich haasten | to hurry |
| zich vervelen | to be bored |
| zich verslapen | to oversleep |
| zich verheugen op | to look forward to |
| zich ergeren aan | to be annoyed by |
| zich wassen / zich aankleden | to wash / to get dressed |
| zich voorstellen | to introduce oneself; to imagine |

- Herinner je je die vakantie nog? — Do you still remember that holiday?
- Hij schaamt zich voor zijn fout. — He's ashamed of his mistake.
- Haast je! — Hurry up!
- De kinderen vervelen zich. — The children are bored.
- We verheugen ons op de vakantie. — We're looking forward to the holiday.
- Mag ik me even voorstellen? — May I introduce myself?
- Ik erger me aan de herrie. — The noise is getting on my nerves.

Some verbs are **always** reflexive (*zich vergissen*, *zich schamen*, *zich haasten*). Others can also have a normal object, and then the meaning changes:

- De kinderen wassen zich. — The children are washing.
- Ik was de auto. — I'm washing the car.
- Ik verveel me. — I'm bored.
- Hij verveelt me. — He bores me.

## In the perfect: always hebben

- Ik heb me vergist. — I was wrong.
- Ik heb me verslapen. — I overslept.
- We hebben ons goed vermaakt. — We had a great time.

## zichzelf: emphasis

The forms with *-zelf* (*mezelf, jezelf, zichzelf, onszelf, uzelf*) stress that it's really the subject and nobody else. They're used with verbs that aren't normally reflexive and after prepositions.

- Ze denkt alleen aan zichzelf. — She only thinks of herself.
- Hij praat tegen zichzelf. — He's talking to himself.
- Ik zie mezelf niet als een kunstenaar. — I don't see myself as an artist.

Don't confuse this with *zelf*, which means "myself / yourself" in the sense of "personally, without help":

- Ik heb de taart zelf gemaakt. — I made the cake myself.

And for "each other", use *elkaar*, not *zich*:

- Ze kennen elkaar al lang. — They've known each other for a long time.

## Common mistakes

Leaving out the pronoun: *Ik herinner niet* should be *Ik herinner het me niet* (I don't remember). Saying *Ik verveel* when you mean you're bored (that means "I bore people"). *Ik ben verveeld* is a common anglicism; say *Ik verveel me*. And with always-reflexive verbs, don't use *-zelf*: *Ik vergis mezelf* is wrong.
