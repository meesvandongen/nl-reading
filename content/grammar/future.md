Dutch has three ways to talk about the future. The good news: the most common one is simply the present tense.

## 1. Present tense + a time word

If it's clear from the context or from a time word that you mean the future, just use the present tense. This is by far the most common way.

- Ik bel je morgen. — I'll call you tomorrow.
- Volgende week gaan we naar Antwerpen. — We're going to Antwerp next week.
- Wat doe je vanavond? — What are you doing tonight?
- De trein vertrekt over vijf minuten. — The train leaves in five minutes.
- Ik kom zo. — I'll be right there.

Useful time words: *straks* (later, in a bit), *zo* (in a moment), *morgen*, *overmorgen* (the day after tomorrow), *vanavond*, *dit weekend*, *volgende week*, *binnenkort* (soon), *later*, *over een uur* (in an hour). If the time word comes first, remember the verb comes second: *Morgen bel ik je.*

## 2. gaan + infinitive

*Gaan* + infinitive is like English "going to": plans, intentions and things you can see coming. The infinitive goes to the end.

- Ik ga vanavond koken. — I'm going to cook tonight.
- We gaan volgend jaar verhuizen. — We're going to move next year.
- Het gaat regenen. — It's going to rain.
- Wat ga je doen in de zomervakantie? — What are you going to do in the summer holidays?

*Gaan* can also mean literally "go (and do)": *Ik ga boodschappen doen* is "I'm going shopping". Often both meanings are there at once.

There is no *te*: *Ik ga te koken* is wrong. And with a place, don't add a second *gaan*: "I'm going to go home" is just *Ik ga naar huis*.

## 3. zullen + infinitive

| | zullen |
| --- | --- |
| ik | zal |
| jij / je | zult or zal |
| u | zult or zal |
| hij / zij / het | zal |
| wij / jullie / zij | zullen |

*Jij zult* is a bit more formal; *jij zal* is normal in speech. In questions: *Zul je…?* or *Zal je…?*

*Zullen* is not the everyday future. It is used for:

**Promises and offers**

- Ik zal je helpen. — I'll help you.
- Het zal niet meer gebeuren. — It won't happen again.
- Zal ik het raam opendoen? — Shall I open the window?

**Suggestions** with *zullen we*

- Zullen we naar de film gaan? — Shall we go to the cinema?
- Zullen we morgen even bellen? — Shall we talk on the phone tomorrow?

**Predictions and assumptions**, often with *wel*. With *wel* it can even be about the present: "probably".

- Je zult het zien. — You'll see.
- Hij zal wel moe zijn. — He's probably tired.
- Dat zal wel kloppen. — That's probably right.

**Formal and written announcements**

- De minister zal morgen een verklaring afleggen. — The minister will make a statement tomorrow.

## Which one should I use?

| you want to say | use |
| --- | --- |
| a plan or fact about the future | present tense: *Ik werk morgen.* |
| an intention, "going to" | *gaan*: *Ik ga een fiets kopen.* |
| a promise, offer or suggestion | *zullen*: *Ik zal het doen. Zullen we gaan?* |

## Common mistakes

Translating every English "will" with *zullen* makes your Dutch sound stiff, or as if you're making a solemn promise. *Morgen zal ik werken* sounds odd; say *Morgen werk ik*.

And beware the false friend *willen*: *ik wil* means "I want", not "I will".

- Ik wil naar huis. — I want to go home.
- Ik ga naar huis. — I'm going home.
