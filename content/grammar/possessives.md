Possessive pronouns (my, your, his…) are simple in Dutch: most of them never change. There is only one form that depends on *de* or *het*: *ons/onze*.

| owner | possessive | unstressed | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ik | mijn | m'n | my |
| jij | jouw | je | your |
| u | uw | — | your (formal) |
| hij, het | zijn | z'n | his, its |
| zij (she) | haar | d'r | her |
| wij | ons / onze | — | our |
| jullie | jullie | je | your (plural) |
| zij (they) | hun | — | their |

- Dit is mijn broer. — This is my brother.
- Waar is je jas? — Where's your coat?
- Is dit uw tas? — Is this your bag?
- Hij belt zijn moeder. — He's calling his mother.
- Ze zoekt haar telefoon. — She's looking for her phone.
- Jullie kamer is boven. — Your room is upstairs.
- Ze bezoeken hun opa. — They're visiting their grandpa.

## ons or onze?

*ons* is used with singular het-words, *onze* with de-words and all plurals. It's the same pattern as *welk/welke*.

- Ons huis is klein. — Our house is small.
- Onze auto is oud. — Our car is old.
- Onze buren zijn aardig. — Our neighbours are nice.

## jouw or je?

*je* is the normal, unstressed form; use *jouw* for emphasis or contrast. With *jullie*, the short form *je* is used once *jullie* has already been said.

- Is dit jouw pen of mijn pen? — Is this your pen or my pen?
- Hebben jullie je kaartjes? — Do you have your tickets?

Don't confuse *jouw* (your) with *jou* (you, object): *Dit is jouw boek* but *Ik geef het boek aan jou*. Dutch people mix these up in writing too.

## Mine, yours: van mij, van jou

To say "mine", "yours" and so on, Dutch usually uses *van* + object pronoun: *van mij*, *van jou*, *van u*, *van hem*, *van haar*, *van ons*, *van jullie*, *van hen*.

- Die fiets is van mij. — That bike is mine.
- Is die tas van jou? — Is that bag yours?
- Van wie is deze sleutel? — Whose key is this?

There's also a more formal form with an article: *de mijne*, *het mijne*, *de jouwe*, *het onze*. It's used when the noun is left out.

- Jouw idee is beter dan het mijne. — Your idea is better than mine.

For people's names, use *van* or add *-s* (without an apostrophe, unless the name ends in a vowel). In speech, you'll also hear *z'n* and *d'r* after a name.

- Dat is de auto van mijn ouders. — That's my parents' car.
- Dat is Peters fiets. — That's Peter's bike.
- Ik heb Anna's boek. — I have Anna's book.
- Dat is Jan z'n jas. — That's Jan's coat. (informal)

## m'n, z'n, d'r

In speech, *mijn* is usually pronounced "mun" and *zijn* "zun"; in informal writing and in dialogue in books, they're written *m'n* and *z'n*. *d'r* for *haar* is very informal. In formal writing, use the full forms.

- Ik ben m'n sleutels kwijt. — I've lost my keys.
- Hij is z'n telefoon vergeten. — He forgot his phone.

## Common mistakes

Using *zijn* for a woman: the possessive matches the **owner**, as in English. *Anna belt haar moeder*, not *zijn moeder*. Confusing *hun* and *haar*: *hun* is only "their", and *haar* only "her". Mixing up *ons* and *onze* (*onze huis* should be *ons huis*).

For things, *zijn* means "its", for both de-words and het-words.

- Het bedrijf heeft zijn kantoor in Leiden. — The company has its office in Leiden.
