Dutch questions are easier than English ones: there's no "do". You just change the word order.

## Yes/no questions: verb first

Put the conjugated verb at the start, then the subject.

- Heb je tijd? — Do you have time?
- Woon je in Amsterdam? — Do you live in Amsterdam?
- Spreekt u Engels? — Do you speak English?
- Is de winkel open? — Is the shop open?
- Kun je me helpen? — Can you help me?
- Heeft hij al gegeten? — Has he eaten yet?

Remember that *jij/je* loses the *-t* after the verb: *Woon je?*, not *Woont je?* Other verbs, infinitives and participles stay at the end, as in normal sentences (*Heeft hij al gegeten?*).

To answer "yes" to a negative question, use *jawel* or *toch wel*: *Heb je geen honger?* (Aren't you hungry?) *Jawel!* (Yes, I am!)

In speech, Dutch speakers often add *toch* or *hè* to check something, like English "right?" or "isn't it?".

- Je komt toch ook? — You're coming too, aren't you?
- Mooi weer, hè? — Nice weather, isn't it?

## Question words

| Dutch | English | example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wie | who | *Wie is dat?* |
| wat | what | *Wat doe je?* |
| waar | where | *Waar woon je?* |
| wanneer | when | *Wanneer begint de film?* |
| hoe | how | *Hoe heet je?* |
| waarom | why | *Waarom lach je?* |
| welk / welke | which | *Welke trein?* |
| hoeveel | how much, how many | *Hoeveel kost dat?* |
| hoe laat | (at) what time | *Hoe laat is het?* |
| van wie | whose | *Van wie is deze tas?* |
| wat voor | what kind of | *Wat voor auto heb je?* |

The question word comes first, then the verb, then the subject.

- Wie is die man? — Who is that man?
- Wat ga je vanavond doen? — What are you going to do tonight?
- Waar is de wc? — Where is the toilet?
- Wanneer kom je terug? — When are you coming back?
- Hoe gaat het met je? — How are you?
- Waarom ben je zo laat? — Why are you so late?
- Hoeveel kost een kaartje? — How much is a ticket?
- Hoe laat vertrekt de trein? — What time does the train leave?

## welk or welke?

*welk* is used with singular het-words, *welke* with de-words and with all plurals.

- Welk boek lees je? — Which book are you reading?
- Welke fiets is van jou? — Which bike is yours?
- Welke schoenen koop je? — Which shoes are you buying?

## waar … naartoe, waar … vandaan

With a preposition, Dutch often splits the question word: *waar* goes first, and the rest goes to the end of the sentence.

- Waar ga je naartoe? — Where are you going?
- Waar kom je vandaan? — Where are you from?
- Waar wacht je op? — What are you waiting for?
- Waar denk je aan? — What are you thinking about?

You can also keep them together, which sounds a bit more formal: *Waarheen ga je?*, *Waarover praat je?*

For **things**, Dutch doesn't say *wat* + preposition. "What are you writing with?" is *Waarmee schrijf je?* or *Waar schrijf je mee?*, never *Met wat schrijf je?* (Note that *met* becomes *mee* here.) For **people**, use the preposition + *wie*.

- Met wie ga je naar het feest? — Who are you going to the party with?
- Aan wie geef je dat cadeau? — Who are you giving that present to?

## Indirect questions

In an indirect question ("Do you know where…?"), the question becomes a subordinate clause and the verb goes to the end. For a yes/no question, use *of* (whether).

- Weet u waar het station is? — Do you know where the station is?
- Ik vraag me af of ze komt. — I wonder whether she's coming.

## Common mistakes

Using a form of *doen* like English "do": *Doe je werken?* should be *Werk je?* Forgetting to put the verb before the subject after a question word: *Waar jij woont?* should be *Waar woon jij?* Using *welk* with a de-word (*welk fiets*). And *Over wat praat je?* instead of *Waar praat je over?*
