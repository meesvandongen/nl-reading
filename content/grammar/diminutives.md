Dutch loves diminutives. By adding *-je* (or a variant) to a noun, you make it small, cute, or simply more casual. You'll hear them all day: *een biertje*, *een kopje koffie*, *een broodje*, *even een momentje*.

## The endings

The basic ending is *-je*. Which variant you need depends on how the word ends.

| ending | when | examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -je | after most consonants | *het huis → huisje*, *het boek → boekje*, *de hond → hondje* |
| -tje | after a vowel, and after *l*, *n*, *r*, *w* following a long vowel, a diphthong or an unstressed syllable | *de koe → koetje*, *de stoel → stoeltje*, *de tuin → tuintje*, *de vrouw → vrouwtje*, *de lepel → lepeltje* |
| -pje | after *m* following a long vowel, a diphthong or an unstressed syllable, and after *lm*, *rm* | *de boom → boompje*, *het raam → raampje*, *de bezem → bezempje*, *de film → filmpje* |
| -etje | after *l*, *m*, *n*, *ng*, *r* following a short stressed vowel | *de man → mannetje*, *de bal → balletje*, *de ring → ringetje*, *het ding → dingetje* |
| -kje | after unstressed *-ing* | *de koning → koninkje*, *de woning → woninkje* |

With *-etje*, the consonant is doubled to keep the vowel short: *man → mannetje*, *bal → balletje*. A final *a*, *o* or *u* is doubled before *-tje*: *de oma → omaatje*, *de auto → autootje*, *het menu → menuutje*.

## Always het, plural always -s

Every diminutive is a **het-word**, even if the original word is a de-word. And the plural always ends in **-s**.

| base word | diminutive | plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| de tafel | het tafeltje | de tafeltjes |
| de man | het mannetje | de mannetjes |
| het huis | het huisje | de huisjes |

This affects the words around it: *dat tafeltje* (not *die*), *een klein huisje* (no *-e*, because it's een + het-word), *het kleine huisje*.

## What it means

**Small:** *een huisje* (a little house, a cottage), *een hondje* (a small dog).

**Cute or affectionate:** *een katje* (a kitten, a sweet little cat), *mijn zusje* (my little sister). With family members, *zusje* and *broertje* usually mean a younger sister or brother.

**Just normal, casual Dutch:** this is the use that surprises learners most. A *biertje* isn't a small beer, it's just "a beer". Ordering *een kopje koffie* or *een broodje kaas* is the normal way to say it.

- Wil je een kopje koffie? — Would you like a cup of coffee?
- Zullen we een biertje drinken? — Shall we have a beer?
- Ik neem een broodje kaas. — I'll have a cheese roll.
- Ze wonen in een klein huisje aan zee. — They live in a little house by the sea.
- Mijn zusje is acht jaar. — My little sister is eight.
- Het meisje speelt met een balletje. — The girl is playing with a little ball.
- Wacht even, een momentje! — Hold on, just a moment!
- Zullen we een rondje lopen? — Shall we go for a little walk?

**A different meaning:** some diminutives have become words of their own. *Het meisje* (girl) has no normal-size form in modern Dutch. *Een broodje* is a roll or sandwich, *een toetje* is a dessert, *een tientje* is a ten-euro note, *een etentje* is a dinner out with friends, and *een beetje* means "a bit".

**Belittling:** a diminutive can also make something sound unimportant: *een baantje* is a small (often temporary) job, not a career.

Diminutives even appear on a few adverbs: *eventjes* (just for a moment), *zachtjes* (quietly), *netjes* (neatly).

- Praat een beetje zachtjes, de baby slaapt. — Talk quietly, the baby's asleep.

## Flemish -ke

In spoken Flemish (and in the south of the Netherlands), diminutives often end in *-ke* or *-eke* instead of *-je*: *een pintje* is the standard word for a glass of beer in Flanders, but you'll also hear *een manneke* or *een boterhammeke*. Think of the famous *Manneken Pis* in Brussels. In written standard Dutch, use *-je*.

## Common mistakes

Using *de*: it's *het huisje*, never *de huisje*. Choosing the wrong ending (*mannje* instead of *mannetje*). A plural in *-en*: it's *huisjes*. And in strict grammar, *meisje* is a het-word, so it's *het meisje dat daar staat*. In speech, many Dutch speakers say *het meisje die*, because a girl is a person; in writing, *dat* is the safe choice.
