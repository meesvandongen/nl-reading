Dutch conversation is full of small, unstressed words like *even, maar, toch* and *wel*. They don't change the facts of a sentence, but they change its **tone**: friendlier, more insistent, surprised, reassuring. They're called *modale partikels*, and English usually expresses the same thing with intonation, "just", "do", a question tag, or a completely different phrase. You don't need to use them all at once, but recognising them will help you understand what people really mean, and *even* and *maar* alone will make your Dutch sound a lot friendlier.

## even: "just (quickly)"

*Even* makes a request or an action sound small and quick.

- Kun je even helpen? — Could you give me a hand?
- Wacht even. — Hang on a sec.
- Ik ga even naar de winkel. — I'm just popping to the shop.
- Mag ik even langs? — Excuse me, can I get past?
- Even kijken. — Let me see.

Don't confuse it with English "even" (that's *zelfs*). *Even* also means "equally": *Ze zijn even oud* means "They're the same age".

## maar: "go ahead", "just"

*Maar* gives permission or encouragement. It can also sound resigned.

- Kom maar binnen. — Come on in.
- Zeg het maar. — What can I get you? / Go ahead.
- Neem er nog maar een. — Go on, have another one.
- Laat maar. — Never mind.
- Dan doen we het maar zo. — We'll just do it that way, then.
- Had ik dat maar geweten! — If only I'd known!

## toch: "surely", "after all", "anyway"

Unstressed *toch* appeals to what the other person knows or should agree with. Stressed *toch* means "anyway, still".

- Je komt toch ook? — You're coming too, aren't you?
- Dat weet je toch? — You know that, don't you?
- Ik heb het toch gezegd! — I told you so!
- Ga toch zitten! — Oh, do sit down!
- Het regende, maar we zijn toch gegaan. — It was raining, but we went anyway.

## hè: "isn't it?", "right?"

*Hè* at the end of a sentence asks for agreement, like an English question tag. At the start, it can express disappointment.

- Mooi weer, hè? — Lovely weather, isn't it?
- Je vergeet het niet, hè? — You won't forget, will you?
- Hè, wat jammer! — Oh, what a shame!

In Flanders you'll often hear *hé* instead.

## wel: "actually", "really", "it'll be fine"

*Wel* contrasts with a negative (said or unsaid), and unstressed *wel* often reassures or softens.

- Ik ben niet moe, maar ik heb wel honger. — I'm not tired, but I am hungry.
- Het komt wel goed. — It'll be fine.
- Hij komt wel. — He'll come, don't worry.
- Dat is wel duur. — That is quite expensive, though.
- Ik denk het wel. — I think so.
- Ja, dat zal wel. — Yeah, right.

## eens: "why don't you", "just"

*Eens* (often pronounced *'ns*) makes a suggestion or invitation sound casual.

- Kijk eens! — Look!
- Probeer het eens. — Give it a try.
- Kom eens langs. — Drop by some time.
- Wat zal ik eens koken? — Now, what shall I cook?

*Eens* also means "once" (*Er was eens* is "Once upon a time"), and *het eens zijn* means "to agree".

## nou: "well", "come on"

*Nou* is the informal form of *nu* (now), but as a particle it adds impatience, emphasis or surprise. It's typical of the Netherlands; Flemish speakers use it much less.

- Nou, ik ga maar eens. — Well, I'd better be off.
- Wat is dat nou? — What on earth is that?
- Kom nou! — Oh, come on!
- Nou en? — So what?
- Dat is nou jammer. — Now that's a real shame.

## gewoon: "just", "simply"

*Gewoon* means "normal", but as a particle it means "just, simply", making something sound obvious or no big deal.

- Zeg het gewoon. — Just say it.
- Ik heb gewoon geen zin. — I just don't feel like it.
- Het is gewoon te duur. — It's simply too expensive.
- Doe maar gewoon. — Just act normal.

*Doe maar gewoon* is almost a national motto. The full saying is *Doe maar gewoon, dan doe je al gek genoeg*: "Just act normal, that's crazy enough." It reflects a Dutch dislike of showing off: don't make a fuss, don't think you're better than anyone else.

## Combining them

Particles are often stacked, in a fairly fixed order.

- Kom eens even hier. — Come here a moment.
- Ik ga maar eens naar huis. — I think I'll head home.
- Doe toch maar niet. — Actually, better not.
- Doe het nou maar gewoon. — Just do it, will you?

## Tips

Particles are unstressed and come after the verb and any pronouns: *Kun je het even pakken?* (Could you grab it?). Translate their effect, not the word: *Kom maar binnen* isn't "Come but inside". And when a Dutch request sounds a bit blunt to you, listen again: there's often a friendly *even* or *maar* in it.
