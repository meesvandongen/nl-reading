English has *this/these* and *that/those*. Dutch has four words, because it also looks at *de* or *het*.

| | near (this, these) | far (that, those) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| de-word | deze man | die man |
| het-word | dit huis | dat huis |
| plural | deze mannen, deze huizen | die mannen, die huizen |

Since all plurals are de-words, the plural is always *deze* or *die*, also for het-words: *dit huis*, but *deze huizen*. Diminutives are het-words, so it's *dit huisje* and *dat meisje*.

- Deze trein gaat naar Utrecht. — This train goes to Utrecht.
- Die jas is te groot. — That coat is too big.
- Dit boek is spannend. — This book is exciting.
- Dat huis is heel oud. — That house is very old.
- Deze appels zijn lekker. — These apples are tasty.
- Die schoenen zijn duur. — Those shoes are expensive.

You can also use them without a noun, meaning "this one" or "that one". The form still matches the noun you mean.

- Welke jas wil je, deze of die? — Which coat do you want, this one or that one?
- Welk brood neem je, dit of dat? — Which loaf are you taking, this one or that one?

## dit and dat for things in general

When you introduce or point at something with *zijn* ("this is…", "those are…"), Dutch always uses **dit** or **dat**, whatever the gender, and even for people and plurals. The verb agrees with what follows: *dit is*, *dat zijn*.

- Dit is mijn broer. — This is my brother.
- Dat zijn mijn kinderen. — Those are my children.
- Wat is dit? — What is this?

*dat* is also the word for "that" when it refers to an idea, a situation or something someone said. It's very common in short reactions.

- Dat is mooi! — That's beautiful!
- Dat is een goed idee. — That's a good idea.
- Dat weet ik niet. — I don't know.
- Dat klopt. — That's right.
- Dat geeft niet. — That's OK. / Never mind.

## die and dat instead of hij, zij, het

In speech, Dutch speakers often use *die* and *dat* where English uses "he", "she", "it" or "they", especially at the start of the sentence when you've just mentioned someone or something. It makes the sentence sound natural and a bit emphatic.

- Mijn buurman? Die is heel aardig. — My neighbour? He's very nice.
- Die film heb ik al gezien. — I've already seen that film.
- Ken je Lisa? Die woont ook in Leiden. — Do you know Lisa? She lives in Leiden too.

*die* and *dat* are also the relative pronouns ("who", "which", "that"), with the same split: *de man die daar loopt*, *het huis dat te koop staat*.

## hier and daar

To make the difference extra clear, especially when pointing, add *hier* (here) or *daar* (there).

- Deze hier is goedkoper dan die daar. — This one here is cheaper than that one over there.

## Common mistakes

Using *dit* with a de-word: *dit fiets* should be *deze fiets*. Using *dit* or *dat* with a plural: *dit boeken* should be *deze boeken*. And *Deze is mijn broer* or *Die zijn mijn ouders* when introducing someone: it's *Dit is mijn broer* and *Dat zijn mijn ouders*.

One thing that looks confusing but isn't: *dat* is also a conjunction ("that"), so you can get two in a row.

- Ik denk dat dat goed is. — I think that's fine.
