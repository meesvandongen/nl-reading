In English you can say "with it", "about that" or "what for". In Dutch, *het*, *dat*, *dit* and *wat* **can't follow a preposition** when they refer to a thing. Instead, they turn into *er*, *daar*, *hier* or *waar*, and the preposition is attached to the end. These combinations are called *voornaamwoordelijke bijwoorden*, but you'll hear them in nearly every conversation.

## The rule

| instead of | say | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| op het | erop | on it |
| op dat | daarop | on that |
| op dit | hierop | on this |
| op wat? | waarop? | on what? |

It works with nearly every preposition: *erin, eruit, erover, ervan, daarnaast, hiervoor, waarover*…

- Ik denk erover na. — I'm thinking about it.
- Ik ben er blij mee. — I'm happy with it.
- Wat doe je daarmee? — What are you doing with that?
- Hiermee kun je de deur openmaken. — You can open the door with this.
- Ik hou ervan. — I love it.

For **people**, the normal preposition + pronoun stays: *aan hem*, *met haar*, *voor jou*.

- Ik denk vaak aan hem. — I often think about him.
- Ik denk eraan. — I'll remember. / I'm thinking of it.

*Aan het*, *met het* and *over dat* (for a thing) are always wrong.

## met → mee, tot → toe

Two prepositions change their form: *met* becomes *mee*, and *tot* becomes *toe*. *Naar* usually becomes *naartoe*.

- Waarmee betaal je? — What are you paying with?
- Ik ben ermee gestopt. — I've stopped. / I've given it up.
- Waar ga je naartoe? — Where are you going?
- Dat leidt nergens toe. — That leads nowhere.

The last example shows that *ergens* (somewhere, something), *nergens* (nowhere, nothing) and *overal* (everywhere, everything) work the same way:

- Hij is overal bang voor. — He's afraid of everything.
- Ik heb nergens zin in. — I don't feel like doing anything.

## Splitting

In a sentence, *er*, *daar* and *waar* usually split off from the preposition. *Er/daar/waar* stay near the front, and the preposition moves to the end, just before the verbs at the end, a bit like English "What are you waiting **for**?"

- Daar heb ik niet aan gedacht. — I didn't think of that.
- Ik heb er geen zin in. — I don't feel like it.
- Waar heb je het over? — What are you talking about?
- Waar wacht je op? — What are you waiting for?
- Daar ben ik het niet mee eens. — I don't agree with that.
- Ik heb er lang over nagedacht. — I've thought about it for a long time.

With a separable verb, the preposition comes before the whole verb: *Ik denk er nog over na.* In questions, the split version is what people say; the joined version (*Waarover heb je het?*) sounds more formal or written.

## er, daar or hier?

*Er* is the neutral, unstressed "it". *Daar* is "that", with emphasis or contrast, and it's what you use at the start of a sentence. *Hier* is "this". *Waar* is for questions and relative clauses (*het huis waarin ik woon*; see the article on relative clauses).

- Ik weet er niets van. — I don't know anything about it.
- Daar weet ik niets van. — I don't know anything about that.

## Pointing ahead

*Er* + preposition can also point forward to a whole clause with *dat* or *om … te*:

- Ik kijk ernaar uit om je weer te zien. — I'm looking forward to seeing you again.
- Ik ben ervan overtuigd dat het lukt. — I'm convinced it'll work.

## Learn the preposition with the verb

Which preposition you need depends on the verb or adjective, and it's often not the English one. Learn them as a package: *denken aan* (think of), *wachten op* (wait for), *houden van* (love), *zin hebben in* (feel like), *praten over* (talk about), *bang zijn voor* (be afraid of), *blij zijn met* (be happy with). Once you know *wachten op*, "What are you waiting for?" is easy: *Waar wacht je op?*
