Many Dutch verbs consist of a short **prefix** and a verb: *op + bellen = opbellen* (to call up), *mee + nemen = meenemen* (to take along). Like English phrasal verbs ("call up", "take along"), the prefix is part of the meaning. And like in English, the two parts often split up.

| verb | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| opbellen | to call (on the phone) |
| meenemen | to take along, to bring |
| aankomen | to arrive |
| opstaan | to get up |
| uitgaan | to go out |
| terugkomen | to come back |
| afspreken | to arrange, to agree to meet |
| opruimen | to tidy up |
| schoonmaken | to clean |

The infinitive is written as one word, and the **stress is always on the prefix**: OPbellen, MEEnemen, AANkomen.

## Main clauses: the prefix goes to the end

In a main clause, the verb part is conjugated and stands in second place; the prefix goes to the **end** of the clause.

- Ik bel je morgen op. — I'll call you tomorrow.
- De trein komt om tien uur aan. — The train arrives at ten.
- Ik sta elke dag om zeven uur op. — I get up at seven every day.
- Neem je een paraplu mee? — Are you taking an umbrella?
- We gaan vanavond uit. — We're going out tonight.
- Ruim je kamer op! — Tidy your room!

Don't forget the prefix: it changes the meaning. *Ik sta om zeven uur op* is "I get up at seven", but *Ik sta om zeven uur* is just "I'm standing at seven".

## Together again

In all other forms, the two parts are written together.

| form | example |
| --- | --- |
| infinitive (after a modal, *gaan*…) | *Ik moet morgen vroeg opstaan.* |
| subordinate clause (verb at the end) | *…omdat ik morgen vroeg opsta.* |
| with *te*: prefix + te + verb | *Ik probeer vroeg op te staan.* |
| past participle: prefix + ge + verb | *Ik ben vroeg opgestaan.* |

- Ik wil je morgen opbellen. — I want to call you tomorrow.
- Ik weet niet hoe laat de trein aankomt. — I don't know what time the train arrives.
- Vergeet niet je moeder op te bellen. — Don't forget to call your mother.
- Ik heb geen tijd om op te ruimen. — I don't have time to tidy up.
- Heb je je paspoort meegenomen? — Did you bring your passport?
- We zijn gisteren laat aangekomen. — We arrived late yesterday.

Note where *te* and *ge-* go: in the middle, not at the front. *op te bellen*, *opgebeld*; never *te opbellen* or *geopbeld*.

## Inseparable prefixes

The prefixes **be-, ge-, her-, ont-, ver-** and **er-** never separate. They are not stressed (beTAlen, verTELlen), and the past participle gets **no extra ge-**.

| verb | meaning | participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| betalen | to pay | betaald |
| vertellen | to tell | verteld |
| herhalen | to repeat | herhaald |
| ontmoeten | to meet | ontmoet |
| gebeuren | to happen | gebeurd |
| erkennen | to acknowledge | erkend |

- Hij vertelt een verhaal. — He's telling a story.
- Ik heb de rekening al betaald. — I've already paid the bill.
- Wat is er gebeurd? — What happened?
- Ik heb haar in Leiden ontmoet. — I met her in Leiden.

A common mistake is *geverteld* or *gebetaald*: it's *verteld* and *betaald*.

## Both: the stress decides

A few prefixes (*door-, over-, onder-, om-, voor-, achter-*) can be separable or inseparable. Some verbs even exist both ways, with a different meaning. The stress tells you which one it is.

| separable (stress on prefix) | inseparable (stress on verb) |
| --- | --- |
| VOORkomen: to occur, happen. *Dat komt vaak voor.* Participle: *voorgekomen* | voorKOmen: to prevent. *Dat kunnen we voorkomen.* Participle: *voorkomen* |
| OVERkomen: to come over (to visit). *Mijn zus komt uit Canada over.* | overKOmen: to happen to someone. *Dat is mij nog nooit overkomen.* |
| ONDERgaan: to go down, to set. *De zon gaat onder.* | onderGAAN: to undergo. *Hij moet een operatie ondergaan.* |

- Dat komt vaak voor. — That happens a lot.
- Zo kunnen we problemen voorkomen. — That way we can prevent problems.
- De zon gaat om negen uur onder. — The sun sets at nine.

## Tips

Learn separable verbs as one word (*opbellen*, not *bellen op*), and when you read a sentence, always check the end for a little word like *op*, *aan*, *mee* or *uit*: it may completely change the meaning of the verb at the start.
