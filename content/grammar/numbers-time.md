## Numbers 0–20

| 0–6 | 7–13 | 14–20 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 nul | 7 zeven | 14 veertien |
| 1 een | 8 acht | 15 vijftien |
| 2 twee | 9 negen | 16 zestien |
| 3 drie | 10 tien | 17 zeventien |
| 4 vier | 11 elf | 18 achttien |
| 5 vijf | 12 twaalf | 19 negentien |
| 6 zes | 13 dertien | 20 twintig |

When *een* means "one" and could be confused with the article "a", it's written *één*: *Ik heb één broer* (I have one brother).

## Tens, hundreds, thousands

| 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 | 80 | 90 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dertig | veertig | vijftig | zestig | zeventig | tachtig | negentig |

Note *dertig* and *veertig* (not *drietig*, *viertig*), and the extra *t* at the start of *tachtig*. Then *honderd* (100), *duizend* (1000), *een miljoen*, *een miljard*. You don't need *een* before *honderd* and *duizend*: *honderd euro*.

**The unit comes before the ten**, joined with *en* (and), as in old English "four-and-twenty":

| 21 | eenentwintig |
| --- | --- |
| 22 | tweeëntwintig |
| 35 | vijfendertig |
| 48 | achtenveertig |
| 99 | negenennegentig |
| 125 | honderdvijfentwintig |
| 2026 | tweeduizend zesentwintig |

Numbers are written as one word up to *duizend*; after *duizend* comes a space. After *twee* and *drie* you need the diaeresis: *tweeëntwintig*, *drieëndertig*. Years before 2000 are usually said in hundreds: 1990 is *negentienhonderdnegentig*, or simply *negentien negentig*.

Dutch uses a **comma for decimals** and a **dot for thousands**: 2,5 is *twee komma vijf*, and 1.000 is a thousand. Prices: €2,50 is *twee euro vijftig*.

**Ordinals** (first, second…) add *-de* up to 19 and *-ste* from 20: *tweede*, *vierde*, *tiende*, *twintigste*, *honderdste*. The irregular ones are *eerste* (1st), *derde* (3rd) and *achtste* (8th).

- Ik ben vijfendertig jaar. — I'm thirty-five.
- Dat kost twee euro vijftig. — That's two euros fifty.
- We wonen op nummer achtentwintig. — We live at number twenty-eight.
- Ze woont op de derde verdieping. — She lives on the third floor.

## Telling the time

*Hoe laat is het?* (What time is it?) *Het is drie uur.* (It's three o'clock.) The key to Dutch time is *half*: **half drie means 2:30**, "half (on the way) to three". The minutes around the half hour are counted from the half.

| time | Dutch |
| --- | --- |
| 3:00 | drie uur |
| 3:05 | vijf over drie |
| 3:15 | kwart over drie |
| 3:20 | tien voor half vier |
| 3:25 | vijf voor half vier |
| 3:30 | half vier |
| 3:35 | vijf over half vier |
| 3:40 | tien over half vier |
| 3:45 | kwart voor vier |
| 3:50 | tien voor vier |

In Flanders, 3:20 and 3:40 are usually *twintig over drie* and *twintig voor vier*; you'll hear that in the Netherlands too, but *tien voor half vier* is more common there. In speech, people use the 12-hour clock and add *'s ochtends* (in the morning), *'s middags* (in the afternoon), *'s avonds* (in the evening) or *'s nachts* (at night). Timetables use the 24-hour clock: *14.32 uur* is *veertien uur tweeëndertig*.

- Hoe laat is het? — What time is it?
- Het is half drie. — It's half past two.
- Ik kom om kwart over acht. — I'll come at a quarter past eight.
- De film begint om tien voor half negen. — The film starts at twenty past eight.
- Ik sta 's ochtends om zeven uur op. — I get up at seven in the morning.
- De winkel gaat om zes uur dicht. — The shop closes at six.

The most common learner mistake: hearing *half drie* and showing up at 3:30, an hour late. Also note that it's *drie uur* (three o'clock) with singular *uur*; *twee uur* can mean "two o'clock" or "two hours".

## Days and months

Days and months are written **without a capital letter**.

| days | months |
| --- | --- |
| maandag, dinsdag, woensdag, donderdag | januari, februari, maart, april |
| vrijdag, zaterdag, zondag | mei, juni, juli, augustus |
| | september, oktober, november, december |

Use *op* for days and *in* for months: *op maandag*, *in mei*. Because *juni* and *juli* sound similar, many Dutch speakers say "juni" and "julij" to tell them apart.

- Ik werk op maandag en dinsdag. — I work on Mondays and Tuesdays.
- Mijn verjaardag is in mei. — My birthday is in May.
- Tot zaterdag! — See you on Saturday!

## Dates

The day comes before the month: *17 oktober*, written in numbers as 17-10-2026 or 17/10/2026. You say the date with a normal number (*zeventien oktober*) or with an ordinal (*de zeventiende*).

- De hoeveelste is het vandaag? — What's the date today?
- Het is vandaag zeventien oktober. — Today is the seventeenth of October.
- Ik ben geboren op 3 juni 1990. — I was born on 3 June 1990.
- Op 27 april vieren we Koningsdag. — On 27 April we celebrate King's Day.
