In a subordinate clause, all the verbs go to the end. With two, three or even four verbs, they form a cluster (*werkwoordelijke eindgroep*), and Dutch has rules, and some freedom, for their order. Main clauses have a cluster too, made of all the verbs except the conjugated one in second place.

## Modal + infinitive

With a modal verb (*kunnen, moeten, mogen, willen*) and an infinitive, the conjugated modal normally comes first.

- Ik weet niet of ik morgen kan komen. — I don't know if I can come tomorrow.
- Ze zegt dat ze vroeg moet opstaan. — She says she has to get up early.

The other order (*of ik morgen komen kan*) also exists, but it's much less common. With a separable verb, the prefix can also go in front of the cluster, which is very common in speech:

- Ze zegt dat ze vroeg op moet staan. — She says she has to get up early.

## Auxiliary + participle: two orders

With *hebben* or *zijn* + participle, both orders are correct and common.

- Ik denk dat ik hem gisteren heb gezien. — I think I saw him yesterday.
- Ik denk dat ik hem gisteren gezien heb. — I think I saw him yesterday.

Dutch grammarians call these the "red" order (*heb gezien*, auxiliary first) and the "green" order (*gezien heb*, participle first). In the Netherlands, the red order is more common in speech; in Flanders, the green order is preferred. In writing you'll see both everywhere. Pick one and be consistent.

## The double infinitive

This is the big one. When a modal verb (or *laten, gaan, blijven, zien, horen, helpen, leren*) is used in the perfect together with another verb, it doesn't become a participle. It stays an **infinitive**. Grammarians call this the *infinitivus pro participio*: "infinitive instead of participle".

- Ik heb het niet kunnen doen. — I wasn't able to do it.
- We hebben lang moeten wachten. — We had to wait a long time.
- Hij heeft het niet willen zeggen. — He didn't want to say it.
- Ik heb mijn haar laten knippen. — I've had my hair cut.
- Ik heb hem zien vallen. — I saw him fall.
- We zijn gaan zwemmen. — We went swimming.
- Ze is blijven slapen. — She stayed the night.

So *Ik heb het niet gekund doen* and *Ik heb gemoeten werken* are wrong. The participles *gekund, gemoeten, gewild* only appear when the modal stands on its own, without another verb:

- Ik heb het niet gekund. — I wasn't able to.
- Dat heb ik nooit gewild. — I never wanted that.

*Te* disappears too. *Hoeven* and the position verbs *zitten, staan, liggen, lopen* lose their *te* in the perfect:

- Gelukkig heb ik niet hoeven wachten. — Luckily I didn't have to wait.
- Ik heb de hele avond zitten lezen. — I spent the whole evening reading.

Which auxiliary? With *gaan* and *blijven* it's *zijn*. With modals it's usually *hebben*, though you'll also hear *zijn* when the main verb normally takes *zijn*: *Hij is/heeft moeten vertrekken*.

## Three verbs in a subordinate clause

In the most common order, the conjugated verb comes first and the main infinitive last.

- Het spijt me dat ik niet heb kunnen komen. — I'm sorry I couldn't come.
- Ik weet niet of hij het boek heeft willen lezen. — I don't know whether he wanted to read the book.

With modal + auxiliary + participle, there are three correct orders. The middle one is especially common in Flanders:

| order | example |
| --- | --- |
| modal, auxiliary, participle | dat hij het moet hebben gelezen |
| participle, modal, auxiliary | dat hij het gelezen moet hebben |
| modal, participle, auxiliary | dat hij het moet gelezen hebben |

- Ze denkt dat hij het boek gelezen moet hebben. — She thinks he must have read the book.

## Main clauses

In a main clause, the conjugated verb takes second place and the rest of the cluster goes to the end, in the same order.

- Ik had het moeten weten. — I should have known.
- Je had het me kunnen vertellen. — You could have told me.
- We zouden morgen kunnen gaan. — We could go tomorrow.

## Rules of thumb

Put the conjugated verb first in the cluster and you'll almost always be right. Use an infinitive, not a participle, when a modal (or *laten, gaan, zien, horen, blijven*) has another verb with it in the perfect. And don't put anything else inside the cluster, except a separable prefix: *dat ik het niet heb kunnen doen*, not *dat ik heb het niet kunnen doen*.
