Five little words join two main clauses: *en, maar, of, want* and *dus*. They are coordinating conjunctions (*nevenschikkende voegwoorden*). Unlike *omdat* or *dat*, they **don't change the word order**: after them comes a normal main clause, with the verb in second place.

| conjunction | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| en | and |
| maar | but |
| of | or |
| want | because, for |
| dus | so |

- Ik drink koffie en mijn vriend drinkt thee. — I drink coffee and my friend drinks tea.
- Het is laat, maar ik ben nog niet moe. — It's late, but I'm not tired yet.
- Gaan we fietsen of nemen we de bus? — Are we cycling or taking the bus?
- Ik neem een jas mee, want het is koud. — I'm taking a coat, because it's cold.
- Het regent, dus we blijven binnen. — It's raining, so we're staying in.

## The conjunction doesn't count

Think of these words as standing *outside* the clause, in "position zero". The clause after them starts fresh. So if it starts with something other than the subject, you still invert:

- Ik werk vandaag en morgen ga ik naar Brugge. — I'm working today and tomorrow I'm going to Bruges.
- Ik wilde komen, maar helaas was ik ziek. — I wanted to come, but unfortunately I was ill.

Don't invert straight after the conjunction: *maar ben ik moe* is wrong; it's *maar ik ben moe*.

If both clauses have the same subject, you can leave it out the second time, as in English:

- Ik sta om zeven uur op en ga meteen douchen. — I get up at seven and have a shower straight away.

A comma usually goes before *maar*, *want* and *dus*, and usually not before *en* and *of*.

## want or omdat?

Both mean "because", but the word order is different. After *want* you get a main clause; after *omdat* the verb goes to the end.

- Ik blijf thuis, want ik ben ziek. — I'm staying at home because I'm ill.
- Ik blijf thuis omdat ik ziek ben. — I'm staying at home because I'm ill.

There are a few other differences:

A *want*-clause can never come first. You can say *Omdat ik ziek ben, blijf ik thuis*, but not *Want ik ben ziek, …*

To answer the question *waarom?* (why?), use *omdat*:

- Waarom ben je te laat? — Why are you late?
- Omdat de trein vertraging had. — Because the train was delayed.

*Want* is also used to explain how you know something, where *omdat* sounds odd:

- Ze is vast thuis, want het licht is aan. — She must be home, because the light's on.

## dus: two word orders

*Dus* can be a conjunction (normal order) or an adverb that takes the first position (then the verb comes right after it). Both are correct and common.

- Het regent, dus we blijven binnen. — It's raining, so we're staying in.
- Het regent, dus blijven we binnen. — It's raining, so we're staying in.

*Dus* can also go in the middle of the sentence:

- We blijven dus binnen. — So we're staying in.

## of: "or" and "whether"

*Of* meaning "or" is a coordinating conjunction. But *of* meaning "whether" starts a subordinate clause, and then the verb goes to the end.

- Kom je morgen of niet? — Are you coming tomorrow or not?
- Ik weet niet of ik morgen kom. — I don't know whether I'm coming tomorrow.

## maar: more than "but"

*Maar* also means "only, just", and it's one of the little words that soften an imperative (*Kom maar binnen*).

- Ik heb maar vijf euro. — I've only got five euros.

## Common mistakes

Putting the verb at the end after *want* (*want ik ziek ben*) is the most common one: that's *omdat* word order. The other is inverting after *en* or *maar* when the subject comes first. Remember: after these five words, simply carry on with a normal sentence.
