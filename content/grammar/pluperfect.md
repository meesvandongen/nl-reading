The pluperfect (*voltooid verleden tijd*) is the Dutch "had done": something that happened **before** another moment in the past. It's also the key to talking about what could have happened but didn't.

## How it's built

Take the perfect tense and put *hebben* or *zijn* in the simple past: *had/hadden* or *was/waren*. The choice between *hebben* and *zijn* is exactly the same as in the perfect, and the participle goes to the end.

| | with hebben | with zijn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ik, jij, u, hij, zij | had gewerkt | was gegaan |
| wij, jullie, zij | hadden gewerkt | waren gegaan |

- Ik had al gegeten. — I had already eaten.
- Ze had het boek nog nooit gelezen. — She had never read the book before.
- We waren de hele dag thuisgebleven. — We had stayed at home all day.

## Before another past event

Use the pluperfect when you're already talking about the past and want to go one step further back. Words like *al* (already), *nog nooit* (never before), *eerder* (earlier), *nadat* (after) and *toen* (when) often go with it.

- Toen we aankwamen, was de trein al vertrokken. — When we arrived, the train had already left.
- Toen ik thuiskwam, had mijn huisgenoot al gekookt. — When I got home, my housemate had already cooked.
- Ik had hem al jaren niet gezien. — I hadn't seen him for years.
- Nadat ze haar huiswerk had gemaakt, ging ze naar buiten. — After she'd done her homework, she went outside.

Dutch is stricter here than English. "After she did her homework, she went out" is fine in English, but after *nadat* with a past main clause, Dutch needs the pluperfect: not *Nadat ze haar huiswerk maakte*.

In reported speech, a perfect becomes a pluperfect:

- Hij vertelde dat hij zijn baan had opgezegd. — He said he had quit his job.

## Things that didn't happen

The pluperfect is the normal way to talk about an unreal past, in both halves of the sentence:

- Als ik het had geweten, had ik het je verteld. — If I'd known, I would have told you.
- Als de bus niet te laat was geweest, was ik op tijd geweest. — If the bus hadn't been late, I'd have been on time.

*Zou hebben* / *zou zijn* + participle is also possible in the result part (*dan zou ik het je hebben verteld*), but the pluperfect is shorter and very common.

Without *als*, you can start with the verb, followed by *dan* in the main clause. This sounds a bit more formal:

- Had je het gevraagd, dan had ik je geholpen. — Had you asked, I would have helped you.

## Wishes and regrets: Had ik maar…

Verb first + *maar* is how Dutch says "if only", "I wish I had".

- Had ik dat maar geweten! — If only I'd known!
- Was ik maar thuisgebleven. — I wish I'd stayed at home.
- Hadden we maar meer tijd gehad. — If only we'd had more time.

## Should have, could have

With a modal verb you get a double infinitive (see the article on verb clusters): *had moeten* (should have), *had kunnen* (could have), *had willen* (would have liked to).

- Dat had je niet moeten doen. — You shouldn't have done that.
- Je had me kunnen bellen. — You could have called me.
- We hadden eerder moeten vertrekken. — We should have left earlier.
- Ik had graag willen komen, maar ik was ziek. — I would have loved to come, but I was ill.

## Common mistakes

Using the perfect where the pluperfect is needed: *Toen ik aankwam, is de trein vertrokken* should be *was de trein vertrokken*. Using the wrong auxiliary: *ik had gegaan* should be *ik was gegaan*, just as in the perfect it's *ik ben gegaan*. And using a participle with modals: *Ik had het gemoeten doen* is wrong; say *Ik had het moeten doen*.
